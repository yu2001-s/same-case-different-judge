# Bibliography

Every source below was found by the literature searches and then checked by an independent reviewer against the publisher, DOI or repository record.
**Confirmed**: details and finding match. **Corrected**: the work exists but a detail was wrong; the fixed details are shown, or the reviewer's correction note is quoted.
Sources the reviewers could not confirm are listed at the end and are not cited in the report.

## Extraneous factors (timing, mood, weather, anchoring, workload): international

- Adi Leibovitch (2016). *Relative Judgments*. Journal of Legal Studies 45(2):281-. [link](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/jls/vol45/iss2/3) — **Confirmed**
  - Finding: Judges judge each case against their own docket. Judges given milder caseloads impose longer sentences and more often use aggravated ranges or upward departures. Judges with graver caseloads are more lenient.
- Andreas Glockner (2016). *The irrational hungry judge effect revisited: Simulations reveal that the magnitude of the effect is overestimated*. Judgment and Decision Making 11(6):601-610. [link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S1930297500004812/type/journal_article) — **Confirmed**
  - Finding: Favorable rulings take longer. A minimally forward-planning rational judge who ends sessions rather than starting likely-long cases produces a drop of similar size. One methodological detail of the original analysis (session censoring) inflates the artifact even without foresight. Conclusion: the original analyses are not conclusive evidence of extraneous influence.
- Arnaud Philippe, Aurélie Ouss (2018). *'No Hatred or Malice, Fear or Affection': Media and Sentencing*. Journal of Political Economy 126(5):2134-2178. [link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-699210.html) — **Corrected**
  - Finding: Sentences are about 3 months longer when the verdict follows coverage of crime. The effect tracks media exposure to crime, not crime itself, and fades quickly. Professional experience and expertise appear to reduce the influence.
- Birte Englich, Thomas Mussweiler, Fritz Strack (2006). *Playing dice with criminal sentences: The influence of irrelevant anchors on experts' judicial decision making*. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 32:188-200. [link](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167205282152) — **Confirmed**
  - Finding: Sentences were pulled toward even blatantly random anchors, and experience did not reduce the effect. Mechanism: selective accessibility, since high anchors raised access to incriminating arguments (Study 4). Earlier Englich & Mussweiler (2001, J. Applied Social Psychology 31:1535-1551) found trial judges given a 34-month vs 2-month prosecutor demand in a rape vignette sentenced about 28.7 vs 18.8 months.

- Daniel L. Chen, Tobias J. Moskowitz, Kelly Shue (2016). *Decision Making Under the Gambler's Fallacy: Evidence from Asylum Judges, Loan Officers, and Baseball Umpires*. Quarterly Journal of Economics 131(3):1181-1242. [link](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/131/3/1181/2590011) — **Confirmed**

## Judge identity and inter-judge disparity: international

- Adam N. Glynn, Maya Sen (2015). *Identifying Judicial Empathy: Does Having Daughters Cause Judges to Rule for Women's Issues?*. American Journal of Political Science 59(1): 37-54. [link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/amposc/v59y2015i1p37-54.html) — **Confirmed**
  - Finding: Holding the number of children constant, judges with daughters vote in a more feminist direction on gender-related issues. The effect is driven mainly by Republican-appointed judges and appears in gender-related civil cases but not in criminal cases such as sexual assault (per press coverage).
