# Literature review: do judgments depend on the judge?

Scope: evidence that court outcomes depend on things the law treats as irrelevant (the judge's identity, mood or circumstances, and the litigant's identity), with a focus on Taiwan. Every work cited here passed an independent verification check; full details and verification notes are in [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md).

## 1. The judge lottery is the most robust finding

When comparable cases are assigned to judges at random, outcomes still differ a lot by judge.

- **US federal sentencing.** Anderson, Kling & Stith (1999) measured inter-judge disparity before and after the Sentencing Guidelines; disparity fell under mandatory guidelines. Yang (2014) found that it rose again once the guidelines became advisory after *Booker*.
- **Asylum.** Ramji-Nogales, Schoenholtz & Schrag (2007), "Refugee Roulette", documented very large grant-rate differences between immigration judges in the same court.
- **State criminal courts.** Abrams, Bertrand & Mullainathan (2012) showed that judges in Cook County differ in their racial sentencing gaps, using random assignment.
- **Career judiciaries.** In the Czech Republic, between-judge disparities shrink as judges gain experience (Drápal & Pina-Sánchez 2023). In Poland, 248 trainee judges who shared the same centralised training and the same case file imposed penalties ranging sixfold (Uhl & Mamak 2026).

**Implication for Taiwan.** Taiwan combines centralised training with only advisory sentencing tools, so sizeable inter-judge disparity is the reasonable prior, and the pilot confirms it for drunk driving.

## 2. Judge identity matters in narrower ways

- **Gender.** Boyd, Epstein & Martin (2010) find that judge sex affects outcomes in only one of 13 areas of US appellate law (sex discrimination). Harris & Sen (2019) conclude in a review that ideology matters much more than race or gender. In France, women receive shorter sentences than comparable men (Philippe 2020).
- **In-group bias.** It appears in Israeli small-claims and criminal courts (Shayo & Zussman 2011; Grossman et al. 2016) and in Kenya (Choi et al. 2022). It reverses in Louisiana juvenile courts (Depew, Eren & Mocan 2017). It is a precise zero across 5 million Indian cases (Ash et al. 2025).
- **Politics.** Republican-appointed federal judges give longer sentences to Black defendants than Democratic appointees (Cohen & Yang 2019).
- **Prior career.** Defendants assigned to US judges with public-defender experience are less likely to be incarcerated (Harris & Sen 2026). Revolving-door lawyers in China win more often (Liu et al. 2025).
- **Career incentives in civil-law judiciaries.** Ramseyer & Rasmusen (1997, 2001) argued that Japanese judges who defied the government were punished in their postings. Fukumoto & Masuyama (2015) re-analysed the data and found no promotion penalty.

## 3. Extraneous factors: dramatic claims tend to shrink

| Claim | Original | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Parole grants fall before meal breaks ("hungry judges") | Danziger, Levav & Avnaim-Pesso (2011) | Case order was not random, since unrepresented prisoners go last (Weinshall-Margel & Shapard 2011). Rational scheduling reproduces the curve (Glöckner 2016). The depletion story is rejected (Daljord, Urminsky & Ureta 2019). The finding is overused in legal writing (Chatziathanasiou 2022). A US bail study finds only a small pre-meal dip and no recovery after meals (Shroff & Vamvourellis 2022). |
| Harsher sentences after the daylight-saving change | Cho, Barnes & Guanara (2017) | Model-dependent, and absent in 2004–2016 data (Spamann 2018) |
| Hot days reduce asylum grants | Heyes & Saberian (2019) | Data errors; the estimate falls by two-thirds and reverses in a longer sample (Spamann 2022). Near-zero in 2.8 million Australian cases (Evans & Siminski 2020). |
| Football losses lengthen juvenile sentences | Eren & Mocan (2018) | Single study |
| Gambler's fallacy in sequential decisions | Chen, Moskowitz & Shue (2016) | Small effects across asylum, lending and baseball umpiring |
| Anchoring on numeric demands | Englich, Mussweiler & Strack (2006) | Meta-analysis gives d ≈ 0.58, with some publication bias (Bystranowski et al. 2021). Prosecutors' demands anchor Korean sentences (Kim & Chae 2017). |
| Media coverage before verdicts | Philippe & Ouss (2018) | Longer sentences in France; the effect is short-lived |

**Taiwan evidence.**
- Month-end case-closing pressure: district judges issue more verdicts and slightly shorter sentences at month-end (Tsai 2022, NTU thesis).
- Commutation law: after the 2007 law, judges raised sentences for commutation-eligible thefts by about 43 days (+25%) and cut sentences for ineligible ones (Huang 2023, NTU thesis).
- Anchoring in civil cases: higher plaintiff claims raise land-related awards, an effect weakened once public price information was released (Chang, Chen, Liao & Lin 2023).

## 4. Taiwan: what is known

Most of the evidence comes from one Academia Sinica / NCKU / Cornell network and from master's theses.

**Courts and judges**
- Appellate reversal depends on ties between appellate and trial judges (Lin & Chang 2025; Wu, Lin & Chang 2026).
- The Supreme Court favours government litigants at the leave stage beyond what party capability explains (Chen, Huang & Lin 2015).
- Constitutional Court studies disagree on ideology. Garoupa, Grembi & Lin (2011) and Dalla Pellegrina, Garoupa & Lin (2012) find no appointing-party effects; Su, Ho & Lin (2018) find circumstantial ideological effects.

**Sentencing**
- Gun cases, 2019: male judges gave about 1.3 months more than female judges, in a correlational analysis (Tseng 2021).
- Death penalty: female, child and elderly victims raise the odds of a death sentence (Shen & Cheng 2016). A machine-learning study of 336 capital cases from 2013–2024 finds prima facie evidence that like cases were not treated alike (Ho, Fan & Chen 2026).
- Sentencing guidance: in Hualien, sexual-offence sentences stayed near statutory minimums after the 2018 reforms, and mitigation and probation became more common. This is a single court with 41 sentences (Lee 2020).

**Damages**
- Non-pecuniary damages follow injury severity but also plaintiffs' demands and the parties' income and status (Chang, Eisenberg, Ho & Wells 2015; Chang, Ho & Hsu 2016).

**Representation**
- Public-defender clients are convicted more often but receive shorter sentences than legal-aid clients (Huang, Chen & Lin 2010).
- Richer and poorer people seek legal advice differently (Huang, Lin & Chen 2014).

**Gap the pilot fills.** No published Taiwanese study was found that uses random case assignment to measure judge-level sentencing disparity.

## 5. Public trust and lay participation

- **Trust.** In the Judicial Yuan's 2025 survey, trust in judges was 59.8%, up from 35.8% in 2016. Only 54% agree that most judges decide independently, and 81% of people reluctant to go to court say they might not get a fair ruling (Judicial Yuan 2025).
- **Why lay participation.** Support for lay participation grew out of dissatisfaction with professional judges (Huang & Lin 2013; Huang & Lin 2014).
- **Citizen judges since 2023.** Evaluations are mostly participant surveys. They report high satisfaction, and they report that prosecutors' sentence requests influenced sentences (Judicial Yuan evaluation committee 2025).

## 6. Methods the field now expects

- **Random-assignment designs and judge-stringency instruments**, with tests of exclusion and monotonicity (Frandsen, Lefgren & Leslie 2023; Chyn, Frandsen & Leslie 2025).
- **Noise-corrected variance components** (Kline, Saggio & Sølvsten 2020).
- **Inference on rankings**, before any judge is labelled lenient or harsh (Mogstad et al. 2024; Gu & Koenker 2023).
- **Multiple-testing corrections and pre-registration** (Anderson 2008; Romano & Wolf 2005), and attention to replication in empirical legal research (Chin & Zeiler 2021).

## 7. Ethics and law

- **France.** France criminalised judge-level analytics in 2019 (Article 33 of the Justice Reform Act), a move criticised as a restriction on research (Langford & Madsen 2019).
- **Taiwan.** Taiwan requires judgments to be published, including judges' names (法院組織法 §83), and the Personal Data Protection Act provides bases for research use of lawfully published data.
- **This project's approach.** The pilot reported judges as anonymous codes. The full report names judges, because their decisions are public by law, but only with reliability safeguards (enough cases, shrinkage, 95% intervals, rank ranges) and without characterising any judge's motives or politics.

## 8. Political prosecutions

- **Inferring partisan targeting from outcomes.** If prosecutors bring weaker cases against the opposition, opposition-linked defendants should be convicted less often and sentenced more lightly (Gordon 2009). We apply this test to Taiwanese election-crime trials around the 2016 change of government.
- **Taiwan context.** Before the 1990s reforms, case assignment and promotion were tools of KMT political control over the courts (Wang 2006), and organised vote buying was a core campaign technique run through local brokers (Wang & Kurzman 2007). Anti-corruption enforcement since then is discussed by Göbel (2016).

## 9. Gap searches

Five further searches covered topics the first round left thin; their sources are verified and listed in `bibliography.md`. Main points:

- **Detention.** Investigation-stage detention rulings are mostly not published, which rules out US-style bail-judge designs. Trial-stage detention, bail and probation decisions are observable with judge names in the microdata.
- **Political cases.** No quantitative study of partisan bias in Taiwan's ordinary courts was found. Case assignment (and reassignment, as in the Chen Shui-bian case and 釋字665) is the documented lever; before the 1990s reforms, assignment and promotion were tools of political control (Wang 2006). Before-and-after comparisons around changes of government are a testable design.
- **Careers.** Training cohort shapes promotion; individual ratings are binary and driven by delay; promotion to second instance relies on peer votes. A judge career panel can be built from personnel decisions (人審會 resolutions, 總統府公報 orders, cohort pages).
- **Reforms abroad.** In Korea, sentencing guidelines removed the earlier effect of having a woman on sex-crime panels (KICJ 2015), and guideline compliance is published annually by court (Sentencing Commission 2025).
- **Marginalised litigants.** In Hualien, all 153 drunk-driving judgments that reduced sentences on grounds of Indigenous culture were written by one judge, in summary proceedings (Fan & Lin 2025). This is judge-dependence in the treatment of an identity group, worth replicating across eastern courts. Foreign defendants at first instance rose from 2,848 (2021) to 7,575 (2025), concentrated in drunk-driving and fraud cases (Judicial Yuan statistics).
